Wealth is a means — not an end.

Investments matter. Tax planning matters. Estate planning matters. But those are ultimately tools — tools that let you live a meaningful life, be there for the people you love, and leave things better than you found them.

Lume is the luminescent paint that makes a watch dial readable in the dark — from the Latin lúmen, the light of life. It’s what a good plan gives you: the ability to see clearly, exactly when it matters most.

Why I do what I do.

When people ask why I became a financial advisor, I don’t think about markets or investments. I think about my parents in the bleachers at my soccer games.

My dad was the first in his family to go to college — he finished dental school at 25 and built his own practice from scratch. My mom had the harder job, raising my siblings and me. They didn’t talk much about money. Instead, they showed me what it was for: being at the games, taking the trips, making being together the priority.

I started college pre-med, switched to finance, and set my sights on investment banking — then graduated in 2009, straight into the financial crisis, and that path disappeared overnight. My mom, who worked for a financial advisor, encouraged me to look at planning. The more I learned, the more I realized I’d found exactly what I wanted: a way to solve complex problems while helping people build the lives they truly want.

Seventeen years and hundreds of families later, I’ve learned that people rarely come to me with a money problem. They come with a life question that money touches.

“Wealth matters — but it’s not the goal. The goal is using it to build a meaningful life, be there for the people you love, and leave things better than you found them.”

That’s why I do what I do.

Christopher M. Moore
Founder & Wealth Management Advisor

Success changes the questions.

The pursuit of success often begins with building wealth. Over time, the questions become less about making more money — and more about making the most of what you’ve built. Those are the conversations we believe matter most.

01 “I spent decades chasing success. Now I have it, and I’m trying to figure out what it actually means.”
Purpose

Success creates freedom. It doesn’t automatically create clarity. We help align your financial resources with the life you want to live, the relationships you want to deepen, and the impact you want to have.

02 “Am I making the most of everything I’ve worked so hard to build?”
Stewardship

Wealth is more than a balance sheet. It’s the product of years of effort, sacrifice, risk, and good decisions. Stewardship means making intentional choices with every resource you’ve been entrusted to manage.

03 “Will my success become an opportunity — or a burden — for the people I love?”
Family

A transfer of wealth is easy. Preparing a family for wealth is harder. We help families build the conversations, values, and structures that let wealth serve future generations rather than define them.

04 “What would change if I had complete confidence that every important financial decision was being handled well?”
Time

The greatest luxury isn’t more wealth. It’s the freedom to spend your attention on family, relationships, health, and the pursuits that give life meaning.

05 “What will my success ultimately make possible?”
Legacy

Legacy isn’t measured by what you leave behind. It’s measured by what continues because you were here. Whether through family, business, philanthropy, or community, we help clients use their resources to create lasting impact.

Those aren’t investment questions. They’re life questions — and they’re the ones we help you answer.

Five questions. Four principles.

These are the commitments behind every recommendation we make — and the standard we hold ourselves to between meetings.

01

Intentionality

Live by design, not by default.

Success creates options. The challenge is deciding which ones are worth pursuing. Money should support a life that reflects your values, priorities, and aspirations — not simply accumulate for its own sake.

02

Stewardship

Honor what you’ve built.

Wealth represents years of hard work, sacrifice, risk, and good judgment. Stewardship means making wise decisions with both the opportunities and the responsibilities that success creates.

03

Presence

Be where you matter most.

The ultimate purpose of wealth isn’t consumption. It’s freedom — the freedom to spend time with family, invest in relationships, pursue meaningful work, and be present for life’s most important moments.

04

Legacy

Leave things better than you found them.

Legacy extends beyond assets. It shows up in stronger families, thriving communities, empowered employees, charitable impact, and values that endure across generations.
